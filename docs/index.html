Setup items to rotate

Things bought for the flat that last: appliances, cookware, crockery, storage, fixtures, keys. When someone moves out, the person who replaces them usually buys out their share. Items marked check are judgement calls, such as the move-in Amazon orders whose contents Splitwise doesn't list.

—’s share of the setup items is — at the chosen valuation, out of — at purchase price. That is the amount the incoming flatmate would pay them to take over their share.

“By seat” follows how the flat has worked: each person who moved out was paid for their share in cash by whoever replaced them, so every current flatmate holds an equal part of anything the whole flat split. Items split between only some people stay with those people. “By the original Splitwise split” counts only what each person was charged when the item was bought.

How the categories and shares were worked out

Categories come from rules on each expense’s description and Splitwise category, applied in order: settle-ups and transfers first, then repairs, staff, services, utilities, milk subscriptions, leisure, groceries and household supplies. Setup items were picked by hand from the descriptions up to the 15 Oct 2026 export; in any newer export, entries whose description looks like something bought for the flat (a cooker, a stand, plates) are marked “check” for someone to confirm. The page reads the Splitwise file in your browser; the rules are in the page’s source on GitHub.

A person’s share of an expense is what Splitwise says they owe for it. The payer’s share is whatever is left of the cost. Seven expenses had two payers; there the leftover is split equally between them.

Straight-line valuation takes the purchase price down evenly to zero over the chosen number of years, counted from the day each item was bought to the move-out date chosen in the statement (today unless changed).
